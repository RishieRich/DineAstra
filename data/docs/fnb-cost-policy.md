# F&B Procurement & Cost Policy

Document owner: Executive Chef
Applies to: Kitchen & Stewarding, F&B Service, Finance
Version: 3.0
Last verified: 16 August 2026

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs how food and beverage inputs are bought, received and
costed, and what the property treats as an acceptable food cost.

## 2. Food cost targets

### 2.1 Standing target

The property's standing food cost target is thirty-one per cent of F&B
revenue, measured daily and reviewed on a trailing seven-day average. A
single day above target is noise; three consecutive days above target is a
variance requiring a written cause.

### 2.2 Escalation threshold

A trailing seven-day food cost more than two percentage points above the
standing target is reported to the General Manager in the daily briefing and
carries a named corrective action.

## 3. Vendor management

### 3.1 Contracted vendors

Produce, dairy, poultry and dry goods are bought on annual contracted rates
from the property's approved vendor list. Purchases outside the approved list
require the Executive Chef's written approval on the requisition.

### 3.2 Rate revisions

Contracted rates are fixed for the contract year except where the contract
carries a supply-disruption clause. A revised rate takes effect only from the
date the revision is countersigned, and never retrospectively.

### 3.3 Produce and dairy rate revision, August 2026

Effective 16 August 2026, the property's primary produce and dairy vendor
invoked the supply-disruption clause in its annual contract following the
monsoon disruption to inbound supply, and renewed at rates approximately nine
per cent above the previous contracted schedule. Property leadership
countersigned the revision on 15 August 2026 on the understanding that it
holds only until the Q4 re-tender. The expected effect on the property's
blended food cost is a step of roughly three to three and a half percentage
points, from the thirty-one per cent standing target to approximately
thirty-four and a half per cent, persisting until the re-tender concludes.
This step is a contracted rate change, not an operational control failure,
and must not be treated as a kitchen variance in the daily briefing.

### 3.4 Re-tender

The Q4 re-tender opens on 1 October 2026 with a target of returning blended
food cost to within one percentage point of the standing target by 1 December
2026.

## 4. Receiving

### 4.1 Verification

Every delivery is checked against its purchase requisition for item, quantity
and unit rate before acceptance. A delivery that does not match its
requisition is either corrected on the spot or refused.

### 4.2 Rate discrepancy

Where the delivered unit rate differs from the contracted rate, the receiving
storekeeper records the discrepancy on the requisition and escalates to the
Executive Chef the same day. Accepting an off-contract rate silently is a
policy breach regardless of the amount involved.

## 5. Daily costing

The daily food-cost entry is completed against the outlet sales report before
the kitchen closes. Costing a day retrospectively from memory is not
permitted; where the sales report is unavailable, the entry is left open and
flagged rather than estimated.
